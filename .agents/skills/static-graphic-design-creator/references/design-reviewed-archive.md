# Design Reviewed Archive

Use this optional external research source when the user requests historical grounding, asks to explore a design movement or period, or the approved visual direction would materially benefit from a specific historical reference. Do not browse it for every ordinary brief. It is supplementary to the brief, this Skill's design atlases, and the optional style-code catalog; it cannot change a concept, copy, reference lock, edit scope, or execution approval.

## Source and access

- Online archive: <https://designreviewed.com/archive/>
- Poster-format collection: <https://designreviewed.com/format/poster/>
- Archive methodology: <https://designreviewed.com/about/archive-methodology/>
- Research and archive access: <https://designreviewed.com/about/research-archive-access/>
- Citing Design Reviewed: <https://designreviewed.com/about/citing-design-reviewed/>

The online archive is a public discovery resource with records browsable through available site filters, subject groupings, and search. The physical collection is broader than the material documented online. Some related research articles require membership. Access is therefore page- and host-dependent; never imply that the complete physical or online archive was inspected.

Check the current host's actual web-search or browser tools before using the archive. A successful web lookup in one session does not prove that ChatGPT Work, Codex, or a later session exposes the same capability. Do not invent a web tool, use a paid service, upload content, or claim that a page or image was inspected when it was not.

## Research workflow

1. Resolve the communication objective, audience, concept, copy, format, and protected properties first. Consult this archive only if the user asks or a concrete design question makes historical research useful.
2. Search by the relevant subject, format, designer, country, decade, or movement. Prefer a specific artefact record over a category page or homepage. Open the record itself and confirm which details it actually provides.
3. Record the artefact title and direct URL. Report designer, date, country, format, and other catalogued facts only when the record supports them. Mark unclear or missing attribution as `Unknown`; do not repair uncertain credits from a visual guess.
4. Analyze observable design properties relevant to the brief: hierarchy, scale, alignment, negative space, image-to-type relationship, palette, geometry, material/process cues, and likely viewing conditions. Separate archive-record facts from visual observations and from your own interpretation. Do not infer physical production details, exact typefaces, or cultural meaning without supporting evidence.
5. Translate a small number of useful properties into an original direction that serves the user's concept and audience. Do not reproduce the composition, artwork, distinctive arrangement, or wording of one artefact. A named designer or movement is context, not permission to imitate a particular work.
6. When the source shaped a recommendation, cite the individual record in the response or handoff and identify the property borrowed at an abstract level. Follow the archive's current citation guidance. A citation credits a record; it does not grant image or reproduction rights.

## Rights and limits

Use archive images as research references only unless the user has separately confirmed the required reproduction permission. Holding a physical artefact or displaying an image online does not establish copyright ownership or reproduction rights. Do not download, bundle, redistribute, or embed archive imagery in the Skill. Do not reproduce long archive text; summarize only what is needed and link to the source.

If browsing is unavailable, a record cannot be opened, or a relevant article is restricted, state the limitation without claiming access. Continue from the Skill's existing references when adequate. Ask the user for a link, screenshot, or excerpt only when the missing source is necessary to meet the request; otherwise proceed without archive research and label historical claims `Unknown` where appropriate.

This archive is not a new slash command, style-code catalog, mandatory onboarding item, or default design mode. User preferences to avoid codes, external research, or a named direction remain in force.
